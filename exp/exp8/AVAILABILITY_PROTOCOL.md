# Availability and observation coverage follow-up

This follow-up preserves the completed benchmark and extension evidence. All
fitting, prediction and statistical evaluation run on AutoDL4.

## Observation coverage audit

Use the existing parking predictions and the original training-defined
blockface/clock-time candidate set. Report planned selections, observed
selections, high-pressure hits and missing outcomes by date, weekday and hour.
Keep December and January separate. The primary display uses the original
500-m, seven-day forecasts and 10% budget; retain the 20% budget sensitivity.

For observed high-pressure count H, observed selected high-pressure count K,
missing selected count M_s and missing unselected count M_u, the sharp binary
outcome recall bounds are K/(H+M_u) and (K+M_s)/(H+M_s). Report the selection
coverage and the empirical selected-event yield alongside conditional recall.
For a paired selection comparison a-b, bound missing-outcome changes in hits
by assigning unknown high pressure to the negative or positive selection
differences. These are identification bounds, separate from sampling intervals.

The original all-day candidate definition is retained for audit. A second,
explicit calendar sensitivity uses weekday/hour/blockface combinations with
at least three observed training dates, excludes Sundays, and excludes the
parking holidays 2020-12-25, 2021-01-01 and 2021-01-18. This is a training-based
observation-availability screen; it represents an explicit evaluation scope.
Both rules are fixed before computing the new comparison statistics.

SDOT metadata published 2018-12-20 describes Monday-Saturday service, no paid
parking on Sundays, varying service hours and occasional missing transaction
records. The historical parking holiday list includes Christmas, New Year
and Martin Luther King Jr. Day. Preserve source URLs and downloaded metadata.

## Public-data release delay

The 2018 SDOT metadata specifies a seven-day public release delay. A forecast
issued before the first target day uses parking observations dated at most
origin minus eight calendar days. Target-date lags of 14 and 21 days satisfy
this bound for leads 1, 3 and 7. The earlier lag-7 experiment remains a
retrospective benchmark.

Validation outcomes end on 2020-12-03 and become available by 2020-12-10 under
this schedule. The first test forecast origin in the availability-aware
comparison is 2020-12-11. Retain the original November 19 fitting cutoff,
validation-only penalty selection and January frozen-fit rule. Record the
information date of every lag and all selection cutoffs.

## Forecast-input mapping

The first availability-aware control retains the observed-visit mapping fit,
changing parking lag availability and evaluation dates. A separate mapping
uses historical POI forecasts generated by models fitted and selected before
the mapping-period outcomes. Its source archive and temporal boundaries will
be fixed before fitting. Compare both mappings on the same final events.

Reuse the already completed Seattle July-cohort GraphGRU, Matched-LSTM and
NLinear-ridge fits. Their training outcomes end on August 19 and their
validation outcomes on September 2. Generate seven-day-ahead forecasts for
October 1-November 19 on the original 9,884-node cohort using the archived
July-December histories. These node-shared models apply to every target through
the same fitted temporal parameters. This step introduces new forecast events
and retains all existing fitted parameters. The final December/January POI
inputs remain the original October-trained models.

Fit the parking map with either contemporaneous observed POI visits or these
earlier-model held-out forecasts. Compare GraphGRU, Matched-LSTM and NLinear
under both map-fitting inputs, using 500 m as the primary radius and 250/1,000 m
as sensitivities. The existing penalty grid and five-seed convention remain
fixed. POI forecasts are supplied at each origin; the availability scenario
audits the publication times of parking records.
